# ISO 24495 plain language

Help the intended reader find, understand, and use the information while preserving the user's purpose, the meaning, and the precision the task needs.

Use four connected principles:

- **Relevance:** give readers the information they need for their purpose, in an amount and context they can use.
- **Findability:** organize, label, and order information so readers can locate what matters.
- **Understandability:** use language and explanations that suit the readers' knowledge without losing needed precision.
- **Usability:** make actions, choices, conditions, and consequences clear enough for readers to use the information.

First identify the readers, their goal, and the decision or action at stake. Preserve required terms, facts, distinctions, and the user's requested voice. Then choose wording, structure, examples, and visual support that serve those needs. Test important or uncertain material with representative readers or a realistic reader-task check when practical.

Do not treat an arbitrary word count, paragraph size, wording ban, heading depth, or output format as an ISO requirement. Use them only when the user, a house style, evidence, or the reader's task justifies them. This skill does not make a clause-by-clause conformity or certification claim.

For general technical and software writing, apply these principles directly. A local software adaptation may name interface terms consistently and make preconditions, actions, results, and recovery paths explicit; it is not an application of Part 3.

## Route by task

Read the references that fit the request. They compose: legal communication, science communication, and document design can all apply to one deliverable. Do not load all references by default.

- For rights, obligations, legal processes, or legal communication for one or more audiences, read [legal communication](references/legal-communication.md).
- For science topics for readers with varied education or background, including students and decision-makers, read [science communication](references/science-communication.md). It covers text and images; specialist scientific peer papers are outside its central focus.
- For a complex document whose navigation, hierarchy, or presentation affects use, read [document design](references/document-design.md).
- For establishing, assessing, or improving an organisation's plain-language practice, read [organisational implementation](references/organizational-implementation.md). Do not use this route for ordinary document edits.
