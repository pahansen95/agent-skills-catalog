# Science communication

Use this reference for scientific topics presented to readers with varied education, background, or decision-making responsibility. It is especially useful for explanations that bridge specialists and students, public readers, or policy and organisational decision-makers. It covers text and images; it does not set a general standard for software or technical documentation.

Establish what the reader needs to know or decide before choosing the depth. Explain essential concepts, units, and methods in the order the reader needs them. Retain uncertainty, limitations, assumptions, evidence strength, and distinctions that affect interpretation. Prefer a familiar explanation when it remains accurate; add the technical term when readers need it to connect with the science.

Use a figure, diagram, table, or worked example when it makes a relationship, scale, comparison, or process easier to understand. Give every visual a clear purpose, label it precisely, and explain its relevance in the surrounding text. Check that the text and visual say the same thing and that neither suggests certainty beyond the evidence.

For specialist peer communication, follow the conventions, methods reporting, and review expectations of the relevant scientific field as well as these reader-centred principles.
