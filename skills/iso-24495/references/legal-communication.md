# Legal communication

Use this reference when a reader needs to understand rights, obligations, a legal process, or the practical effect of legal text. Identify each audience and their purpose; a public explanation, contract party, adviser, and decision-maker can need different views of the same rule.

Preserve the legal meaning. Keep a defined term or other term of art when it carries necessary legal precision, and explain it in ordinary language where that helps the reader. Do not make a legal conclusion or rewrite that changes rights, liability, jurisdictional effect, or the user's approved legal position without the necessary authority and review.

Make the operational structure visible: who does what, when, under which conditions, using what channel, and what follows. Put exceptions and deadlines near the rule they qualify. Break a complex procedure into its actual stages when that helps a reader act. Use headings that describe the reader's question or task, and keep cross-references, defined terms, and citations accurate.

Choose modal language and formatting that fit the jurisdiction, document type, and established drafting convention. Do not impose a blanket ban on legal terms or assume a word choice has one legal effect everywhere. When an oral explanation accompanies a legal process, align it with the authoritative record and say where the reader can obtain the controlling text.
